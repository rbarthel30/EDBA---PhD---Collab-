# Manuscript Outline

**Working title:** Strategic Disclosure and Capital Market Responses to FDA Comment Letters in the Medical Device Industry

**Author:** Armando Cuello (lead) · Robert Barthelemy (methods)

---

## 1. Introduction
- Motivating question: when the FDA flags a medical device firm for compliance or quality issues, how does the firm choose to disclose, and how does the market react?
- Why FDA letters are interesting in their own right (distinct from SEC letters): they target product safety, manufacturing quality, and clinical efficacy — not financial reporting.
- Contribution: extends the SEC-comment-letter disclosure literature to a regulator whose scrutiny has direct product-market and reputational consequences.

## 2. Institutional Background
- Types of FDA communications: 483, Untitled Letter, Warning Letter — what each means and when each is issued.
- The medical device regulatory pathway: 510(k) vs. PMA vs. de novo.
- Mandatory vs. voluntary disclosure obligations when a firm receives FDA scrutiny.
- *(Armando leads — anchor on lived industry detail.)*

## 3. Hypothesis Development
- **H1 (disclosure timing):** Firms receiving more severe FDA letters disclose faster via 8-K.
- **H2 (capital market reaction):** Short-window CAR is negative around letter disclosure; magnitude varies with letter severity.
- **H3 (strategic shaping):** Firms strategically frame disclosures to dampen negative reaction — language, timing, bundling with positive news.
- **H4 (cross-sectional):** Reaction is stronger for smaller firms, single-product firms, and firms with concentrated institutional ownership.
