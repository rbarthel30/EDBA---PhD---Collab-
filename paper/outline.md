# Manuscript Outline

**Working title:** Strategic Disclosure Responses to FDA Comment Letters in the Medical Device Industry

**Author:** Armando Cuello

---

## 1. Introduction
- Motivating question: when the FDA flags a medical device firm for compliance or quality issues, **how does the firm strategically shape what, when, and how it discloses?**
- Why FDA letters are distinct from SEC letters: they target product safety, manufacturing quality, and clinical efficacy — areas where managerial discretion over disclosure framing has direct product-market and reputational stakes.
- Contribution: extends the SEC-comment-letter disclosure literature to a regulator whose scrutiny operates outside the financial-reporting domain, where strategic disclosure incentives are stronger and the legal/regulatory disclosure obligations are murkier.

## 2. Institutional Background
- Types of FDA communications: Form 483, Untitled Letter, Warning Letter — what each means, when each is issued, and which are publicly observable.
- The medical device regulatory pathway: 510(k) vs. PMA vs. de novo.
- What disclosure obligations actually apply when a medical device firm receives FDA scrutiny — and the wide zone of managerial discretion that exists between "must disclose" and "may disclose."

## 3. Hypothesis Development

The empirical question is **strategic disclosure choice** in the presence of regulator scrutiny. Timing and capital-market reaction enter as supporting evidence that disclosure choices have real consequences and are made deliberately.

### Primary hypotheses — strategic disclosure choice

- **H1 (disclosure choice).** Following an FDA comment letter, firms make strategic choices over *whether* to disclose, *what* to disclose, and *through which channel* — they do not mechanically pass the letter through to the market.
- **H2 (framing).** Conditional on disclosing, firms strategically frame the disclosure to dampen negative interpretation — choice of language, level of specificity, placement (8-K vs. MD&A vs. press release vs. earnings call), and degree of voluntary remediation detail.
- **H3 (bundling).** Firms strategically bundle FDA-letter disclosures with offsetting positive news (product launches, earnings beats, repurchase announcements) to attenuate the market response.
- **H4 (cross-sectional variation in strategic behavior).** Strategic shaping is more pronounced among firms with stronger incentives or capacity to manage disclosure — larger firms, those with concentrated institutional ownership, those with sophisticated IR/legal functions, single-product firms with more at stake per letter.

### Secondary hypotheses — supporting evidence

- **H5 (timing).** Firms with stronger incentives to manage perceptions delay first public acknowledgment of the letter, or accelerate it to bury under unrelated news.
- **H6 (capital market response).** Short-window cumulative abnormal returns (CAR) around the *firm's chosen disclosure date* are negative on average, and the magnitude varies with disclosure framing — establishing that the strategic choices in H1–H4 have economic consequences.

## 5. Empirical Strategy

**Primary analyses (strategic disclosure):**
- Logit / multinomial logit: P(disclose | letter), P(channel chosen | disclose)
- Text-based measures of disclosure framing (tone, specificity, hedging language)
- Bundling tests: probability of co-occurring positive announcement within ±N days of letter disclosure

**Secondary analyses (supporting):**
- Hazard model for time-to-first-disclosure
- Short-window event study (CAR estimation) around firm's chosen disclosure date
- Robustness: alternative windows, factor models, placebo dates
